## Part 1 · Getting around without a car

### 1.1 How buses, shuttles and taxis work in Guatemala

There are no passenger trains. Everything moves by road, plus boats on the lakes and a few domestic flights. Six kinds of transport matter for this trip. This is how the plan chains them together:

<pre class="mermaid">
flowchart LR
    GUA([GUA airport]) -- private transfer --> ANT[Antigua]
    ANT -- tourist shuttle --> ATI[Lake Atitlán]
    ATI -- tourist shuttle --> XELA[Xela]
    XELA -- shuttle, or 2 pullman buses --> COB[Cobán]
    COB -- microbus --> LAN[Lanquín]
    LAN -- tourist shuttle --> FLO[Flores]
    FLO -- tourist shuttle --> RIO[Río Dulce]
    RIO -- pullman bus + Uber --> GUA2([GUA airport])
</pre>

From the most comfortable to the cheapest:

**🚐 Tourist shuttles** (*shuttles*, *transporte turístico*) are the backbone of this plan. They are shared minivans for 10–15 people that run fixed daily routes between the places travellers go (Antigua, the lake, Xela, Lanquín, Flores, Río Dulce) and usually pick you up at your hotel and drop you at or near the next one. Bags go on the roof or in the back. You book a day or more ahead through your hotel, a travel agency in town, or online (for example [Adrenalina Tours](https://www.adrenalinatours.com), [Monteverde Tours](https://monteverdetours.rezdy.com), [Turisteando](https://turisteando.wetravel.com)). A typical fare is US$20–45 (≈ Q150–Q345, €18–€41) per person per leg. Expect them to leave 15–45 minutes late while they collect everyone, and to stop once for food. A **private transfer** is the same thing for just the two of you, at roughly 3–5 times the price; it's worth it at night (13 Dec) and as a fallback.

**🚌 Pullman buses** (*primera clase*) are big coaches with numbered seats and a toilet, run by companies such as Litegua, Fuente del Norte, Monja Blanca and Transportes Álamo, between their own terminals. Tickets are bought at the terminal (often the day before), by phone, or on resale sites such as [Busbud](https://www.busbud.com/en-za/bus-company/litegua). They're cheap (about Q100–Q145 (€12–€17) for a 4–6 h leg) and are the safe way to do the long legs that shuttles don't cover. Their Guatemala City terminals are in Zona 1, so you take an Uber between them.

> **A bit of linguistics:** Guatemalans call a first-class coach a ***pullman***, after George Pullman, the 19th-century American maker of luxury railway sleeping cars, so an English surname became a Spanish common noun. The man who hangs out of the door of a chicken bus collecting fares and shouting the destination is the ***ayudante***, "helper", from Latin *adiutare*, the same root as English **aid** and **adjutant**. And *camioneta*, which means "pickup truck" or "van" in most Spanish-speaking countries, means "bus" in Guatemala.

**🐔 Chicken buses** (*camionetas*, *buses extraurbanos*) are retired US school buses, painted in bright colours, that go everywhere, often, for very little: Q5–Q30 (€0.6–€3.6) for a 1–3 h ride, paid in cash to the *ayudante* on board. **Microbuses** (*micros*, *colectivos*) are minivans that do the same job on shorter or rural routes. They leave when full. Both are a great way to see daily life, but they are crowded, driven fast, and robbery and extortion attacks on them do happen, especially in and around Guatemala City. The US State Department forbids its own staff to use them (or white street taxis) ([US travel advisory, Mar 2026](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/guatemala.html)). This plan uses them **only for short daylight hops** on quiet routes: Xela → Laguna Chicabal, Xela → Zunil (Option 2 on 23 Dec), Cobán → Lanquín, and Río Dulce → Finca El Paraíso. Keep your bags with you or in sight, carry little cash on you, and keep your phone away near the door.

**🛺 Tuk-tuks** (*mototaxis*) are three-wheeled motorbike taxis, everywhere outside the capital: in Antigua, the lake villages, Lanquín, Flores and Río Dulce. They have no meters. **Agree the price before you get in**; a short ride inside a town is usually Q5–Q15 (€0.6–€1.8) per person (my estimate, about 70 % sure), more at night or to a trailhead.

**🚕 Taxis and Uber.** [Uber](https://www.uber.com/gt/en/) works in Guatemala City and Antigua (the main guide quotes ~Q320 (€38) from the airport to Antigua), and I'm about 60 % sure it also works in Xela; inDrive is a common alternative. In Guatemala City, use Uber or the official airport taxi desk, never a taxi hailed on the street. Elsewhere, ask your hotel to call a taxi they know, and agree the fare first. Taxis are how you get to a trailhead or a hot spring when no bus fits, at very roughly Q150–Q400 (€18–€47) for a return trip with waiting time (my estimate).

**⛵ Boats, 🛻 pickups and ✈ flights.** *Lanchas* (public boats) link the villages on Lake Atitlán and the lodges on the Río Dulce. Pickup trucks with benches in the back carry people up the last rough kilometres to Semuc Champey, just as in the car plan. TAG and Avianca fly Guatemala City ↔ Flores in about an hour, from about US$70–110 (≈ Q535–Q840, €63–€100) one way ([Kayak: FRS–GUA fares](https://www.kayak.co.uk/flight-routes/Flores-Santa-Elena-FRS/Guatemala-City-La-Aurora-GUA)); see the [shortcut on 29 Dec](#a-shortcut-fly-from-flores-and-skip-río-dulce).

**Four habits that make it work:**
- **Travel by day.** Every intercity leg in this plan arrives before dark (sunset is ~17:30–17:50), except the 13 Dec arrival, which is a pre-booked private transfer.
- **Book the shuttles 2–3 days ahead in Christmas week**, and confirm the pickup time with the operator the evening before. Some routes need 48–72 h notice.
- **Carry small notes** (Q5, Q10, Q20) for chicken buses and tuk-tuks; drivers rarely have change for Q100.
- **Pack small.** One backpack each (plus the day packs for the volcanoes) fits on any shuttle roof and any tuk-tuk.

**The balance:** €1,450–1,850 not spent, minus €415–870 spent, leaves **≈ €580–1,430 saved**. If you pair like with like (the cheaper car against the cheap options, or the dearer car against the comfortable ones), it's **about €1,000 either way**. That's about €500 per person, or roughly a quarter of the [trip's total cost](../#14-what-the-trip-costs).

**Two smaller savings and one extra cost that aren't in the tables:** the day tour on 28 Dec (Option 1) includes a guide, which the car plan pays for separately (≈ Q300–Q600 (€36–€71) for Tikal and Yaxhá). Hotels in Flores cost about the same as El Remate and El Sombrero. On the other side, if you'd rather not take any chicken bus at all, use the "high" column for 21, 23, 25 and 29 Dec; that adds about Q1,400 (€170).

**What it costs in time and freedom:**
- **~5–7 extra hours on the road** over the trip (my estimate), mostly on 24 Dec (10–11 h instead of 8 h) and 27 Dec (8–9 h instead of 6.5 h, so the afternoon swim at El Remate goes).
- **No night at Yaxhá.** You sleep in Flores both nights; Yaxhá at sunset is still possible with the day tour (Option 1 on 28 Dec).
- **Fixed departure times.** You can't stop at a viewpoint or change plans on the spot; if a shuttle is full, you wait for the next day's.
- **Less stress:** no night drive on the 13th, no speed bumps, no *bloqueos* to route around yourself, no parking or car-return worries on the 30th, and no damage excess.

### 1.6 Hiring a private driver for some regions

A middle way between renting a car and riding shuttles: a **car or minivan with a driver, just for the two of you** (*transporte privado*, *chofer privado*). The driver collects you at your hotel, waits while you stop for a view or lunch, and drops you at the next one. You pay **per vehicle**, not per person. Book through an agency in Antigua, Xela or Flores (Adrenalina and Monteverde Tours both sell private transport), a transfer site such as [Daytrip](https://daytrip.com/en/countries/gt), or your hotel; for a multi-day hire, ask whether the driver's meals and room are included (I'd assume yes in a quote, ~70 % sure). Tip around Q50–Q100 (€5.9–€12) a day.

**Two ways to combine them:**
- **Driver only for the long days** (24 and 27 Dec): ≈ €300–530 extra over the shuttles, which still leaves you roughly €500–700 better off than with the rental car (see [§1.2](#12-what-it-saves-and-what-it-costs)).

A driver for the **whole trip** (17 days at US$180–280 a day ≈ €2,800–4,300) costs far more than the rental car, so I've left it out.

### 2.1 Antigua & Acatenango (13–17 Dec)

#### Sun 13 Dec — land 21:25, transfer to Antigua
**The day's plan**

- **21:25–22:30** ✈ Land at GUA, immigration and bags.
- **22:30–00:00** Choose one (book ahead):
  - **Option 1:** 🚐 a **private transfer**, about US$67 plus a ~US$5 night surcharge (≈ Q550, €65) for the car ([Civitatis](https://www.civitatis.com/en/guatemala-city/transfers), [Viator](https://www.viator.com/en-AU/tours/Guatemala-City/Private-Trasport-Guatemala-City-Airport-to-Antigua/d4595-140323P9)). - **Fallback:** an [Uber](https://www.uber.com/gt/en/) (~Q320 (€38), fewer drivers after midnight) or the 24-hour official taxi desk in arrivals.

I'd take Option 1: after a long flight, a driver who is already waiting is worth the extra ~€35.

### 2.2 Lake Atitlán (17–20 Dec)

#### Thu 17 Dec — shuttle to Lake Atitlán, then wander San Juan
**The day's plan**
- **08:30–12:00** 🚐 **Shared shuttle Antigua → San Pedro La Laguna** (~3–3.5 h), picked up at your hotel. About US$22–25 (≈ Q168–Q191, €20–€23) each; Living in Guatemala lists 08:30 and 14:00 departures, Turisteando 09:00 and 12:00 ([Living in Guatemala: Antigua to San Pedro](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/antigua-to-san-pedro-la-laguna/), [Turisteando](https://turisteando.wetravel.com/trips/shuttle-from-antigua-guatemala-to-san-pedro-la-laguna-turisteando-4577357832)).
- **12:00–12:15** 🛺 Tuk-tuk from San Pedro to your lodge in San Juan La Laguna (5 minutes, ≈ Q10–Q20).
- **12:15–13:30** Check in, lunch.
- **13:30–17:30** 🚶 **Free: wander San Juan La Laguna and enjoy the lake**, as in the [main guide](../#thu-17-dec--antigua--lake-atitlán-then-wander-san-juan): weaving cooperatives, painters' galleries, coffee, the Mirador Kaqasiiwaan at ~17:00, a first swim.
- **Evening:** dinner in San Juan or San Pedro. Ask your lodge to book a tuk-tuk for 04:00 tomorrow, for Indian Nose. Early night.

Some shuttles end at Panajachel instead, across the lake; from there a public lancha to San Juan or San Pedro takes about 25–30 minutes (Q25–Q35 (€3.0–€4.2), my estimate). The direct one to San Pedro saves the boat with luggage.

#### Fri 18 Dec — Indian Nose at sunrise, then swimming 🏊
**The day's plan** (the same as the [main guide](../#fri-18-dec--indian-nose-at-sunrise-then-swimming-); at the lake you get about by tuk-tuk and boat in both plans)
- **04:00–08:00** 🌄 Indian Nose sunrise hike, with a community guide or a tour that collects you.
- **08:00–12:30** Free; choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🛌 breakfast, a nap and a slow morning in San Juan.
  - **Option 2:** 🛶 kayak or paddleboard on the lake, ~09:00–11:00, while the water is calm.
  - **Option 3:** 🪢 **zipline and the butterfly dome at the Reserva Natural Atitlán, Panajachel**: 09:00–09:45 lancha to Panajachel (≈ Q25–Q35 each), a tuk-tuk (Q10 each) or 15-minute walk to the reserve, check in 10:30 for the 11:00 group, zipline and butterflies until 12:30, lunch in Panajachel, then a lancha straight to San Marcos at 13:30. **Q310 (€37)** each for the Cables X-Tremos (1 h 30) or **Q450 (€53)** for the Ultra X-Tremos (2 h), entry included. Details in the [main guide](../#fri-18-dec--indian-nose-at-sunrise-then-swimming-).
- **12:30–13:00** ⛵ Lancha from San Juan to San Marcos (with Option 3, 13:30–14:00 from Panajachel instead).
- **13:00–17:00** 🏊 Swim and jump at Cerro Tzankujil (from ~14:00 with Option 3).
- **~17:00** ⛵ Lancha back before the last boats.
- **Evening:** dinner in San Juan.

#### Sat 19 Dec — Volcán San Pedro (3,020 m) ⛰️
**The day's plan** (as in the [main guide](../#sat-19-dec--volcán-san-pedro-3020-m-))
- **06:00–15:00** Choose one:
  - **Option 1:** ⛰ Volcán San Pedro: tuk-tuk at ~06:00 to the park office in San Pedro, then 4–5 h up and 3–4 h down.
  - **Option 2:** 🚲 the lake-villages bike ride (~08:00–14:00).
- **15:30–17:00** 🏊 Cool-down swim in the lake.
- **Evening:** pay the lodge in cash, pack, and confirm tomorrow's shuttle time to Xela. Early night.

#### Sun 20 Dec — a last lake morning, then shuttle to Xela
**The day's plan**
- **08:00–11:30** Free: a last morning at the lake; choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🏊 a slow breakfast and a last swim, or the kayak if you skipped it on the 18th.
  - **Option 2:** 🧶 a weaving or coffee session in San Juan (MayAchik' workshops from Q65 (€7.7)).
- **11:30–12:15** Check out (cash), an early lunch, then a 🛺 tuk-tuk to San Pedro if the shuttle doesn't collect in San Juan.
- **12:30–15:00** 🚐 **Shared shuttle San Pedro → Xela** (~2–2.5 h), about US$33–40 (≈ Q252–Q306, €30–€36) each ([Monteverde Tours](https://monteverdetours.rezdy.com/384143/shuttle-lake-atitlan-san-pedro-san-marcos-to-xela), [Bookaway](https://www.bookaway.com/routes/guatemala/san-pedro-la-laguna-to-quetzaltenango-xela)). I couldn't confirm departure times: book the one nearest midday, and if only a morning one fits, swap the free morning for an afternoon in Xela.
- **15:00–18:30** 🚶 Free: check in, then wander Xela's centre around Parque Centro América; drop by your Tajumulco operator's office if you want.
- **Evening:** dinner in the centre; taxi back after ~21:00.

The cheap alternative is the boat to Panajachel and a public bus to Xela, about US$11 each and 3–4 hours with changes ([Universal Traveller](https://www.universal-traveller.com/?p=178025)). With bags, the direct shuttle is worth the difference.

### 2.3 Xela & Tajumulco (20–24 Dec)

#### Mon 21 Dec — Laguna Chicabal by bus, then a calm afternoon in Xela
**The day's plan**
- **06:45–07:45** Choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🐔 a **chicken bus or colectivo towards Colomba / Coatepeque**, ~45–60 min, about Q5–Q15 (€0.6–€1.8) each. Ask the *ayudante* for the stop for Laguna Chicabal (*la parada de la laguna*) near San Martín Sacatepéquez; travellers report that colectivos leave from near the Democracia market and chicken buses from near the San Marcos church in the centre ([Along Dusty Roads](https://alongdustyroads.com/posts/visit-laguna-chicabal-guatemala)). From the stop, it's a longer walk to the entrance than by car, so allow an extra 30–60 minutes.
  - **Option 2:** 🚕 a **taxi from Xela** to the entrance, with the driver waiting or coming back at 12:00 (≈ Q300–Q400 (€36–€47) return, my estimate; ask your hotel).
- **08:00–12:30** ⛰ Laguna Chicabal: up, round the crater lake, back down (open 08:00–13:00; Q20 (€2.4) each). No swimming: it's sacred.
- **12:30–13:30** Back to Xela the same way (buses pass often until dark), lunch.
- **13:30–17:30** 🛌 Free: a calm afternoon in Xela before Tajumulco.
- **18:00–19:00** 📋 Tajumulco briefing (Quetzaltrekkers only).
- **Evening:** dinner, pack a small bag for Tajumulco, leave the big bags at the hotel.

One blog says the colectivo doesn't run on Sundays; the 21st is a Monday.

#### Tue 22 Dec — Tajumulco, day 1: hike up to camp ⛰️
**The day's plan** (as in the [main guide](../#tue-22-dec--tajumulco-day-1-hike-up-to-camp-); the operator provides the transport)
Choose one (read the advisory and access boxes in the main guide):
- **Option 1: Tajumulco.**
  - **05:00** Leave Xela with your operator.
  - **~05:00–14:00** 🚌 Transport to Tuichán, then ⛰ the hike up to camp at about 4,000 m.
  - **14:00–20:00 at camp:** rest, eat, stay warm, early night.
- **Option 2: Tajumulco is off, so a free day in Xela.**
  - **09:00–18:00** 🛌 Rest and pack for tomorrow's pre-dawn Santa María hike.

#### Wed 23 Dec — Tajumulco, day 2: summit at sunrise, then hot springs ⛰️♨
**The day's plan**
- **Until ~14:00** Choose one (the same choice as yesterday):
  - **Option 1: Tajumulco.** ~04:00 summit for sunrise, descend, and the operator's ride back to Xela by ~14:00.
  - **Option 2: Volcán Santa María.** ~02:30–11:00, a pre-dawn hike with the operator, who arranges the ride to the trailhead.
- **14:00–14:30** A quick shower at the hotel.
- **14:30–18:00** Choose one:
  - **Option 1:** ♨ **Fuentes Georginas by shuttle.** A collectivo shuttle leaves Xela at 14:30 (another at 09:00) for about US$25 (≈ Q191, €23) each, **entry included** ([Living in Guatemala: Fuentes Georginas](https://livinginguatemala.com/activities/fuentes-georginas/); [Adrenalina's return shuttle](https://www.wetravel.com/trips/from-quetzaltenango-to-the-hot-springs-fuentes-georginas-go-and-return-adrenalina-tours-0203460123) is another). About 2 hours in the pools, back in Xela around dusk.
  - **Option 2:** ♨ **Las Cumbres private steam sauna, by bus.** A chicken bus to Zunil (~30 min, Q5–Q10 each), then a tuk-tuk or the short walk to [Las Cumbres](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Las+Cumbres+Eco-Hotel+Termalismo+y+Spa+Zunil), about Q75 (€8.9) each per hour. Back by bus before dark (~17:30).
- **Evening:** an easy dinner. Confirm tomorrow's shuttle pickup (Option 1, 07:00) or set an alarm for the 05:00 bus (Option 2). Early night.

To reach Fuentes Georginas on your own instead, take the bus to Zunil and a pickup truck up the 8 km to the springs (Q15–Q20 each way; about Q40–Q80 return by public transport in all, plus entry, per the same Living in Guatemala page).

#### Thu 24 Dec — Xela → Cobán, by shuttle or two buses
**The day's plan**
- **The trip's longest travel day.** Choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🚐 **the direct shuttle, 07:00–~17:00.** Adrenalina runs a daily Xela → Lanquín shuttle at 07:00 for US$75 (≈ Q574, €68) each, about 10 hours to Cobán ([Living in Guatemala: Xela to Lanquín](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/xela-to-lanquin-semuc-champey/), Oct 2026). Ask to get off in Cobán. Book 48–72 h ahead, and ask whether it runs on Christmas Eve.
  - **Option 2:** 🚌 **two pullman buses through the capital, 05:00–~15:30.**
    - **05:00–09:00** Transportes Álamo, Xela → Guatemala City, about Q105 (€12) each ([Living in Guatemala](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/guatemala-city-to-quetzaltenango/), Oct 2026).
    - **09:00–10:00** 🚕 Uber across the city to the Monja Blanca terminal (≈ Q50–Q80).
    - **10:00–15:00** Monja Blanca, Guatemala City → Cobán (~4.5–5 h, roughly hourly), Q105–Q125 (€12–€15) each ([Living in Guatemala: Guatemala City to Cobán](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/guatemala-city-to-coban/)). Christmas Eve buses fill up: be at the terminal early, or buy the ticket by phone.
  - **Option 3:** 🚗 **a private driver, 07:00–~15:00**, door to door on the car plan's route (~8 h with stops), ≈ US$250–400 (≈ Q1,900–Q3,060, €225–€365) for the car (my estimate; see [§1.6](#16-hiring-a-private-driver-for-some-regions)). Book it a week or more ahead: drivers want to be home for Nochebuena.
- **15:30–17:30** 🚶 Free (Options 2 and 3): check in, climb the steps to the Templo El Calvario, and 💵 **draw cash** for Lanquín and Semuc. With Option 1, draw cash on arrival and save El Calvario for tomorrow morning.
- **Evening:** 🎄 **Nochebuena:** tamales and *ponche* if your hotel serves them (ask ahead), then the town-wide fireworks at midnight.

No direct public bus links Xela and Cobán ([Rome2rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Quetzaltenango-Xela/Cob%C3%A1n)). Option 1 is simpler and stays in one seat; Option 2 is about Q650 (€75) cheaper for the two of you and arrives earlier, but involves a change in Guatemala City. I'd pick Option 1 if it runs that day, and Option 3 if it doesn't and the budget allows.

### 2.4 Cobán, Lanquín & Semuc Champey (24–27 Dec)

#### Fri 25 Dec — Cobán → Lanquín by microbus 🏊
**The day's plan**
- **07:30–09:00** ☕ Free: a slow Christmas breakfast; El Calvario if you arrived late yesterday.
- **09:00–11:30** Choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🚐 a **microbus from the Cobán market to Lanquín**, Q40–Q50 (€4.7–€5.9) each, 2–2.5 h ([Living in Guatemala](https://livinginguatemala.com/es/transporte/ciudad-de-guatemala-a-coban/)). There's no published timetable, and on Christmas morning there will be fewer; ask your hotel the evening before.
  - **Option 2:** 🚕 ask your Lanquín lodge to send a driver to Cobán (≈ Q500–Q700 (€59–€83) for the car, my estimate).
- **11:30–13:00** Check in, lunch.
- **13:00–16:00** 🏊 River tubing (via your lodge, if it runs on Christmas Day).
- **17:00–18:30** 🦇 Grutas de Lanquín at dusk, a short walk or tuk-tuk from town.
- **Evening:** dinner at the lodge.

**Where to stay:** pick a lodge **in Lanquín town** or one whose own shuttle meets the Flores shuttles. Adrenalina's Lanquín → Flores shuttle (27 Dec) collects only from central Lanquín hotels, not from those near Semuc.

#### Sat 26 Dec — Semuc Champey 🏊
**The day's plan** (the same as the [main guide](../#sat-26-dec--semuc-champey-): the lodges' 4×4 pickups are how everyone gets there)
- **~07:30** 🛻 Lodge pickup truck from Lanquín to Semuc Champey.
- **08:00–16:00** 🏊 K'anba cave first, then El Mirador, then the pools (early or late, to avoid the 10:30–15:00 crowds).
- **~16:00** 🛻 Pickup back to Lanquín.
- **Evening:** 🛌 free, at the lodge. Pack; the shuttle leaves at 08:00 tomorrow.

#### Sun 27 Dec — Lanquín → Flores by driver or shuttle
**The day's plan**
- **08:00–16:00** Choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🚐 **shared shuttle Lanquín → Flores** (~8 h, via the Sayaxché ferry), about US$40–45 (≈ Q306–Q344, €36–€41) each. Adrenalina's leaves at 08:00 and stops at the "I ❤ Petén" letters at the entrance to Flores ([Living in Guatemala: Lanquín to Flores](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/lanquin-to-flores/)).
  - **Option 2:** 🚗 **a private driver, 08:00–~15:30**, US$325 (≈ Q2,490, €295) for the car (same source), straight to your hotel. You could also go on to El Remate for the afternoon swim, as in the car plan ([§1.6](#16-hiring-a-private-driver-for-some-regions)).
- **16:00–16:30** 🛺 Tuk-tuk onto the island, check in (with Option 2 the driver drops you at the hotel).
- **16:30–17:45** 🚶 Free: walk round the island's lakeside promenade at sunset, and 💵 **draw cash** for Tikal (no ATMs there).
- **18:00–21:00** 🍽 Dinner in Flores. Confirm tomorrow's pickup time. Early night.

This day replaces the drive to El Remate and its afternoon swim; you get a swim in Flores tomorrow afternoon with Option 2, or at the lodge in Río Dulce on the 29th.

### 2.5 Flores & Tikal (27–29 Dec)

Without a car, **Flores** is the better base: it's where the shuttles and the tours start and end, and it has the restaurants and ATMs. You sleep there on 27 and 28 Dec, instead of El Remate and the Yaxhá lodge. For what Tikal and Yaxhá are, tickets and guides, see [Tikal & Yaxhá in the main guide](../#25-tikal--yaxhá-2729-dec).

#### Mon 28 Dec — Tikal, with or without Yaxhá
**The day's plan**
- **Choose one** (book either a few days ahead; buy the Tikal ticket online in advance in both cases):
  - **Option 1: 🏛 Tikal + 🌅 Yaxhá at sunset in one day tour, ~07:00–~19:30** (times per operator). Adrenalina Tours runs it from Flores for about US$55 (≈ Q420, €50) each, with transport and a guide ([adrenalinatours.com](https://www.adrenalinatours.com)); entry fees are extra. You reach Tikal after the 06:00 opening, so you'll share the Gran Plaza with more people, but you see both sites and the sunset from Temple 216.
  - **Option 2: 🏛 Tikal at opening, without Yaxhá.**
    - **~04:30–06:00** 🚐 the early shared shuttle from Flores to Tikal (≈ Q150 (€18) each return, my estimate; Rome2rio lists US$8–50 one way ([Rome2rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Flores-Santa-Elena/Tikal))).
    - **06:00–12:30** 🏛 Tikal: Temple IV, the Gran Plaza, Mundo Perdido, the Central Acropolis. Hire a guide at the gate.
    - **12:30–14:00** 🚐 the midday shuttle back to Flores.
    - **14:00–17:30** 🏊 Free: a swim off a dock in Lake Petén Itzá, or a lancha ride round the island.
- **Evening:** dinner in Flores. Early night: the shuttle to Río Dulce leaves at 06:00.

**If you want the Yaxhá lodge anyway:** ask [Ecolodge El Sombrero](https://www.travelocity.com/Flores-Hotels-El-Sombrero-Ecolodge.h132844709.Hotel-Information) whether it can arrange a car from Tikal and back to the highway on the 29th; I'd expect that to cost several hundred quetzales each way, and it makes the 29th harder, so I've left it out.

#### Tue 29 Dec — Flores → Río Dulce by shuttle + hot waterfall 🏊
**The day's plan**
- **06:00–10:00** 🚐 **Shared shuttle Flores → Río Dulce** (~4 h), US$23–36 (≈ Q176–Q275, €21–€33) each. Living in Guatemala found three agencies with a 06:00 departure, dropping off at the Colifato coffee shop in the Mirador del Río mall ([Living in Guatemala: Flores to Río Dulce](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/flores-to-rio-dulce/)).
- **10:00–10:30** ⛵ Your lodge's boat or a tuk-tuk to the lodge (many river lodges collect guests from town).
- **10:30–12:00** Check in, lunch.
- **12:00** 🎫 On the way back through town, **buy tomorrow's bus ticket** at the Litegua office.
- **12:30–13:30** 🚐 Colectivo towards El Estor from below the bridge, about Q15 (€1.8) each; ask to be let off at Finca El Paraíso ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/guatemala/attractions/turicentro-el-paraiso/a/poi-sig/1331618/358403)).
- **13:30–15:45** 🏊 Finca El Paraíso hot waterfall (Q15–Q20 entry; closes 17:00).
- **15:45–16:45** 🚐 Colectivo back to Río Dulce. **The last one passes at about 16:30–17:00**, so don't cut it fine; or ask your lodge to arrange a taxi for the return.
- **Evening:** 🛌 free, at the lodge: a swim from the dock, dinner on the river. The last night of the trip.

#### A shortcut: fly from Flores and skip Río Dulce
If the bus days start to wear you down, stay a third night in Flores (29 Dec) and **fly Flores → Guatemala City on the 30th** instead (TAG or Avianca, about an hour, from about US$70–110 (≈ Q535–Q840, €63–€100) each one way ([Kayak](https://www.kayak.co.uk/flight-routes/Flores-Santa-Elena-FRS/Guatemala-City-La-Aurora-GUA), [Travelocity](https://www.travelocity.com/es/lp/flight-routes/avianca-from-mundo-maya-intl-to-la-aurora-intl/av/frs/gua))). That replaces about Q650–Q1,100 (€77–€130) of shuttles, buses and Ubers with Q1,070–Q1,680 (€127–€200) of flights, and you land at the airport itself, so no transfer is needed. You'd lose the hot waterfall and the river. This shortcut isn't in the calendar.

### 2.6 Río Dulce & back to the airport (29–31 Dec)

#### Wed 30 Dec — Río Dulce → Guatemala City by pullman, fly at 02:30
**The day's plan**
- **07:30–09:00** Free; choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🏊 a morning swim from the lodge's dock.
  - **Option 2:** 🏰 the Castillo de San Felipe (opens 08:00), 3 km from town by tuk-tuk.
- **09:00–09:30** ⛵ Back into town, to the Litegua stop.
- **09:30–15:30** 🚌 **Litegua pullman, Río Dulce → Guatemala City** (~6 h), Q120–Q145 (€14–€17) each in *Primera Plus* ([Living in Guatemala: Litegua prices 2026](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/litegua-bus-prices-2026/)). Departure times differ between sources (09:30 is the one most listed, with others at night); confirm them when you buy the ticket on the 29th. Fuente del Norte runs the same road if Litegua is full.
- **15:30–23:00** 🛏 A shower, dinner and a few hours' sleep before the night flight, in a room booked for the afternoon and evening. Choose one:
  - **Option 1:** 🚕 15:30–16:15 Uber to the [Hilton Garden Inn](https://www.hotelsbyday.com/en/hotels/guatemala/guatemala-city/hilton-garden-inn-guatemala-city-guatemala) in Zona 9 (≈ Q60); 16:15–22:30 day room and dinner there; 22:30–23:00 Uber to the airport (≈ Q40–Q80).
  - **Option 2:** 🚕 15:30–16:30 Uber to a guesthouse under 1 km from the terminal in Zona 13, such as [L'Aurora Inn](https://www.booking.com/hotel/gt/l-39-aurora-inn.html) (free 24-hour airport shuttle) or [Casa Aurora](https://nz.hotels.com/ho3561270976/casa-aurora); neither lists a day rate, so ask, or book the night. 16:30–23:00 rest and dinner, then the shuttle at ~23:00. **I'd lean to this one:** no second ride across the city after dark.
- **23:30** ✈ Be at the terminal.

Buses into the capital can run late in the afternoon traffic, but with the flight at 02:30 you have about 7 hours of slack. In Guatemala City, take Ubers from inside the bus terminal and the hotel lobby, not taxis from the street.

#### Thu 31 Dec — fly home at 02:30 ✈
**The day's plan**
- **00:00–02:30** Airside at La Aurora: check-in and security done, wait at the gate.
- **02:30** ✈ The flight departs.
