## Guatemala in brief

Guatemala is just south of Mexico, about the size of Iceland or the US state of Tennessee. Your trip covers two very different parts of it:

- **The highlands (Dec 13–25):** a spine of mountains and about 37 volcanoes, mostly 1,500–4,200 m high. Days are sunny and around 20–25 °C in December, the dry season. Nights are cold, and at the high camps it gets close to or below freezing. Most highland people are Maya. More than 20 Mayan languages are still spoken (K'iche', Kaqchikel, Mam and others), and Spanish is often a second language. Many women still wear *traje*, handwoven clothing whose patterns show which village they come from.
- **Petén (Dec 26–29):** hot, humid lowland rainforest in the north, where the great ancient Maya cities stood between roughly 200 and 900 AD.

**Practical basics:** the currency is the quetzal (GTQ). Spanish gets you everywhere, and English is common in Antigua and at Lake Atitlán. Bring layers: shorts for Tikal, and a down jacket, hat and gloves for the volcano nights.

## The itinerary

### Antigua (Dec 13–17)
**What it is:** Antigua was Guatemala's colonial capital until earthquakes in 1773 made the Spanish move the capital to today's Guatemala City. What they left behind is a small, walkable town of cobbled streets, pastel houses and ruined churches. Three volcanoes surround it: Agua, Fuego and Acatenango. Fuego is one of the most active volcanoes in the world and puffs ash every few minutes.

- **Dec 13 (Sun) – Arrive.** Pick up the rental car at the airport and drive to Antigua (about 1–1.5 h). If you land after about 16:00, stay near the airport and drive the next morning.
- **Dec 14 – Explore and adjust to the altitude (1,530 m).** See the Santa Catalina Arch and the ruins of La Merced and the cathedral. Walk up to Cerro de la Cruz for the view over the town toward Volcán de Agua.
- **Dec 15–16 – Acatenango overnight hike.** You hike about 5–7 hours up to a camp at around 3,600 m, directly across from Fuego, and watch it erupt lava after dark. At dawn you can climb to the 3,976 m summit. Tours pick you up in Antigua, so leave the car parked. Book now, and check the volcano's status a few days before. Alerts: https://insivumeh.gob.gt
- **Dec 17 – Morning off, then drive to Lake Atitlán (about 2.5–3 h).**

### Lake Atitlán (Dec 17–20)
**What it is:** a deep lake at 1,560 m that fills an ancient volcanic crater, with three volcanoes along its south shore (San Pedro, Tolimán and Atitlán). About a dozen Maya villages line the shore, each with its own character: Panajachel is the busy hub, San Marcos the yoga and wellness village, San Pedro has a backpacker scene, and San Juan La Laguna is quiet and known for weaving and art cooperatives. Small boats called *lanchas* link the villages. I'd stay in **San Juan**, which you can reach by car.

- **Dec 18 – Indian Nose (Rostro Maya) at sunrise.** Start around 04:00, climb steeply for about 45 minutes, and watch the sun rise behind the volcanoes. In the afternoon, kayak or visit San Juan's women's weaving cooperatives.
- **Dec 19 – San Pedro volcano (about 3,020 m).** Plan 4–6 hours round trip through coffee farms and cloud forest, with a local guide.
- **Dec 20 (Sun) – Drive to Chichicastenango (about 2–2.5 h).**

**A bit of linguistics:** *Atitlán* is usually traced to Nahuatl *atl*, "water". The same root is in *ahuacatl*, which reached English as *avocado*.

### Chichicastenango (Dec 20–21)
**What it is:** a K'iche' Maya market town known for one of Central America's biggest markets, every Thursday and Sunday. The white Santo Tomás church was built on top of a pre-Hispanic temple, and its steps are still used for Maya ceremonies. You'll be there for the **Fiesta de Santo Tomás**, which peaks on Dec 21 with processions, masked dances, fireworks and the *palo volador*. Book a room now.

- **Dec 20 – The Sunday market and the eve of the festival.** Stay overnight.
- **Dec 21 – The festival's main day.** See the morning processions and the *palo volador*, then drive to Xela (about 2 h) by mid-afternoon.

**Etiquette:** ask before photographing people, especially during ceremonies, and enter the church only through the main door.

### Xela / Quetzaltenango (Dec 21–25)
**What it is:** Guatemala's second city, at 2,330 m. It's cooler, grittier and far less touristy than Antigua. Everyone calls it **Xela** ("SHEH-la"), short for its K'iche' name *Xelajú*. The official name, Quetzaltenango, is Nahuatl for "place of the quetzal bird".

- **Dec 22–23 – Tajumulco overnight hike.** At 4,220 m, Tajumulco is the highest point in Central America. You camp near the top and reach the summit at sunrise, often above a sea of cloud. Quetzaltrekkers is a good operator: https://www.quetzaltrekkers.com/xela/tajumulco/
- **Dec 24 – Laguna Chicabal and the hot springs.** An easy hike to a crater lake sacred to the Mam Maya (don't swim), then the steaming pools at **Fuentes Georginas**. That night is **Nochebuena**.
- **Dec 25 – Drive back to Antigua (about 4 h).** Roads are quiet on Christmas Day.

### Tikal and Yaxhá, in Petén (Dec 26–29)
**What it is:** the ancient Maya cities of the northern rainforest. **Tikal** was one of the most powerful Maya cities, with pyramid temples more than 60 m tall. **Yaxhá** is smaller and far quieter, between two lagoons. Base yourselves in **Flores**, a tiny island town on Lake Petén Itzá. No car needed: shuttles and tours cover everything.

- **Dec 26 – Return the car at the airport, fly to Flores (about 1 h).** Book the flights soon.
- **Dec 27 – Tikal.** Go early. Climb Temple IV for the classic view of temple tops above the jungle.
- **Dec 28 – Yaxhá.** Few visitors, a beautiful lakeside setting, and the sunset from Temple 216.
- **Dec 29 – Fly back to Guatemala City, then go to Antigua.**

**A bit of linguistics:** the name *Tikal* is modern, often glossed as "at the water hole". The city's ancient name was *Yax Mutal*.

### Wrap-up (Dec 30–31)
- **Dec 30 – Pacaya or a day off.** Pacaya is an easy half-day hike up an active volcano near Antigua.
- **Dec 31 – Fly home.**

## Driving and safety
- **Rent a high-clearance SUV.**
- **Don't drive after dark.**
- **Leave nothing visible in a parked car.** Use guarded parking (*parqueo*).
- **Use Waze as well as Google Maps.**
- **Carry your papers:** passport, licence, rental papers and an International Driving Permit.

Book first: the Acatenango tour, a Chichicastenango room for Dec 20, and the Flores flights for Dec 26 and 29.
