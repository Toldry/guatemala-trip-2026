### 1.2 Guatemala in brief

Guatemala is the country just south of Mexico, a little larger than Iceland. Your trip crosses three very different landscapes:

- **The Western Highlands** (13–24 Dec): mountains at 1,500–4,200 m, about 37 volcanoes, and pine and cloud forest. December is dry and sunny, around 20–25 °C by day. Nights get cold, and the volcano camps drop below freezing. Most people here are Maya, speaking K'iche', Kaqchikel, Tz'utujil, Mam and other languages alongside Spanish. Many women wear *traje*, handwoven clothing whose patterns identify their village.
- **Alta Verapaz** (24–27 Dec): green, misty cloud-forest hills with coffee and cardamom farms. This is Q'eqchi' Maya country.
- **Petén and Izabal** (27–30 Dec): hot, humid lowland rainforest where the great Classic Maya cities stood (roughly 200–900 AD), and the Caribbean side around Río Dulce.

> **A bit of linguistics:** many place names are [Nahuatl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl), the language of the Aztec allies who came with the Spanish conquistadors, rather than Maya. *Quetzaltenango* means "place of the quetzal (bird)". *-tenango* means "place of"; compare *Chichicastenango*, "place of the nettles". *Atitlán* contains *atl*, "water", the same root as in *ahuacatl*, which reached English as **avocado**. Meanwhile, *Xela*, *Semuc Champey* and *Tikal* come from Maya languages. Two layers of naming are visible on the same map.

**Where the money goes:** lodging and food are about half of it, and both are in your hands day by day. The fixed costs are the car (€170 plus any cover), fuel (~€150) and the two volcano treks (~€350–€480).

#### What to ask the rental company
- **Is collision cover (CDW) included or extra?** The Kayak price excludes it. Enterprise in Guatemala, for example, makes its CDW compulsory unless you show written proof that your credit card covers collision and theft there, and its CDW still leaves a deductible of up to US$2,500 depending on the car class.
- **How big is the deposit?** Expect a hold on your credit card of roughly US$800–2,200 depending on the company and car (Enterprise takes at least US$800; Avis US$1,700–2,200). Use a card with enough limit.
- **Is a second driver extra?** Avis, for example, charges US$10 + VAT a day per additional driver, which over 18 days is about US$200 (≈ €180). Ask before you both plan to drive.
- **Third-party liability:** some sites say it became compulsory in May 2025, but according to [Living in Guatemala's insurance guide](https://livinginguatemala.com/driving/rental-insurance-guide/) those rules were repealed days after they were published, before taking effect. Ask whether liability cover is in the price; Avis says its public liability follows the country's insurance laws.

#### Keep the guide on your phones, offline

The guide works **without a connection** once you've opened it on your phone with internet: the page, maps, photos and calendar feed are saved on the phone, and the links in the calendar events open that saved copy. Signal is patchy on the volcanoes, around Lanquín and in Petén, so do this before you fly:

1. **Open the guide on each phone, on Wi-Fi**, and wait a few seconds for the download (about 15 MB, mostly maps and photos).
2. **Install it as an app** (optional, but it gives you an icon and a full-screen view):
   - **Android, Chrome:** menu ⋮ → **Add to home screen** / **Install app**.
   - **iPhone, Safari:** Share → **Add to Home Screen**.
3. **Test it:** switch on airplane mode, then tap a link in one of the calendar events. The guide should open at the right section.

**Two limits:**
- On an **iPhone**, links from Google Calendar open in Safari (or the in-app browser), not in the home-screen icon, and iOS keeps their saved data separately. Open the guide once in **Safari itself** on Wi-Fi as well, so the copy that calendar links reach is saved too. I'm about 75 % confident this covers the in-app browser; test it in airplane mode before you go.
- The saved copy updates the next time you open the guide online. Older versions (the links at the top) are saved only once you've opened them. Outside links (Google Maps, booking sites, Wikipedia) still need a connection.

## Part 2 · Day by day

### 2.1 Antigua & Acatenango (13–17 Dec)

![Arco de Santa Catalina in Antigua, with Volcán de Agua behind it](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Arco_de_Santa_Catalina_Antigua_Guatemala_edit2.jpg?width=1280)
*The Arco de Santa Catalina in Antigua, with Volcán de Agua (3,760 m) behind it.*

**What it is.** [Antigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_Guatemala) was the Spanish colonial capital until the earthquakes of 1773. Today it is a walkable grid of cobbled streets, pastel houses and ruined baroque churches, ringed by the volcanoes Agua, Fuego and Acatenango. It has the best food and services on the trip, which makes it a good base for the first few days.

> **👥 Crowds & 🚗 traffic here**
>
> - 👥 **Antigua centre** gets busy on weekends and in holiday weeks, with day-trippers from Guatemala City, though its cobbled streets have little through-traffic. Go early in the morning for quiet streets and photos at the Arco.
> - 👥 **Acatenango base camps:** in peak season, hundreds of hikers from dozens of operators camp on the same ridge. The summit is busy at sunrise; the views make up for it.
> - 🚗 **Airport ↔ Antigua road** via Guatemala City: heavy traffic at rush hours. At 22:30 on the 13th it will be quieter.

![Map of Antigua, the volcanoes and the airport]({{ "/maps/map-04-antigua.png" | relative_url }})

#### Sun 13 Dec — land 21:25, pick up the car, drive to Antigua
**The day's plan**
- **Before 21:25:** nothing in Guatemala; you're on the plane.
- **21:25–23:00** ✈ Land at GUA, immigration, bags, pick up the rental car.
- **23:00–00:00** 🚗 Drive to Antigua (~1 h) and check in. Straight to bed.

Immigration and bags take 30–60 minutes, then the car pick-up another 30–45 minutes, so expect to drive off around 23:00 and reach Antigua around midnight.

##### Picking up the car at the airport (GUA)
- The rental desks are in arrivals; the cars are in a **separate parking building across from the terminal**, which is slow late at night.
- **Walk round the car and photograph every scratch** (and the fuel gauge) before you leave, and check the spare tyre and jack.
- Confirm the **return procedure for the evening of 30 Dec**: desk hours, or where to leave the keys ([§2.7](#27-río-dulce--the-drive-home-2931-dec)).
- Load the route in **Waze** and download offline maps while you still have airport Wi-Fi.

##### The drive to Antigua (~40 km, about 1 h at night)
**Route:** from the airport, follow the Calzada Roosevelt west out of Guatemala City onto the **CA-1**, past Mixco to **San Lucas Sacatepéquez**, then turn off down the **RN-10** to Antigua. The CA-1 is a busy multi-lane highway, quiet at that hour; the last ~10 km from San Lucas is a steep, winding descent.

Night driving is something this guide otherwise avoids ([§3.5](#35-driving)). This is the one exception, because it's short and on main roads. The specific things to watch for:
- **Unmarked speed bumps** (*túmulos*), unlit vehicles and people walking on the road edge, especially on the descent into Antigua.
- **Keep doors locked and windows up** in Guatemala City, and don't stop on the roadside; if you need anything, wait for a lit petrol station.
- **Police checkpoints** are normal; slow down, be polite, and have your passport, licence and rental papers ready.
- **Tell your Antigua hotel you'll arrive around midnight**, and book one with private parking and someone on the door.

##### Parking in Antigua (13–17 Dec)
**It's cheap.** The car sits in Antigua for 4 days, including the 2 days you're on Acatenango.

- **Best: a hotel with private parking.** Many Antigua hotels include it free; filter for "parking" on [Booking.com: Antigua hotels with parking](https://www.booking.com/parking/city/gt/antigua-guatemala.html). The car then sits safely behind a gate while you're on Acatenango (15–16 Dec). Confirm that the hotel will keep it overnight while you're away.
- **Municipal car parks:** around Q40 (€4.7) a day at most (the cap at the La Pólvora and Santa Inés lots in July 2026). Over the 3–4 days, that's about Q120–Q160 (€14–€19).
- **Street parking: avoid it.** The municipal street charge is Q10 (€1.2) a day, or Q20 (€2.4) at weekends and on holidays (June 2026 rules, currently disputed in court). But payment only covers the car until 23:59 that day, cars left on the street for more than 36 hours can be towed, and the centre has few spaces. A rental car on a dark street also invites break-ins ([§3.5](#35-driving)). Source: [Living in Guatemala: Antigua vehicle rules](https://livinginguatemala.com/antigua-vehicle-restrictions/).

**The day's plan**

### 2.2 Lake Atitlán (17–20 Dec)

![Lake Atitlán seen from the top of Volcán San Pedro](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Volcan-San-Pedro-Panorama.JPG?width=1280)
*Lake Atitlán from the summit of Volcán San Pedro, which you'll climb on the 19th.*

**What it is.** [Atitlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Atitlán) is a deep lake at 1,560 m filling a huge volcanic crater, with three volcanoes on its south shore: San Pedro, Tolimán and Atitlán. A dozen Maya villages line the shore, linked by *lanchas* (small boats that work like water taxis). Your base, **San Juan La Laguna**, is calm and known for its weaving and painting cooperatives. San Pedro (the backpacker village) is next door, and San Marcos (yoga and wellness) is a 10-minute boat ride away.

> **👥 Crowds & 🚗 traffic here**
>
> - 👥🚗 **Panajachel** is the lake's busy hub: tuk-tuks, buses, a long souvenir street and weekend crowds from the capital. You only pass near it.
> - 👥 **San Pedro La Laguna** is the backpacker party village, loud at night and especially at weekends. San Juan next door is much calmer, which is why it's your base.
> - 👥 **Indian Nose** has a small crowd of a few dozen people at sunrise. **Cerro Tzankujil** is busiest on weekend afternoons; Friday the 18th should be fine.
> - 🚗 Villages are linked by **narrow, steep roads with tuk-tuks**. Around the lake, the boats are the pleasant way to get about.

#### Thu 17 Dec — Antigua → Lake Atitlán, then wander San Juan
**The day's plan**
- **Early morning:** breakfast in Antigua, collect the car from the hotel's parking.
- **08:00–11:30** 🚗 Drive about 3 h from Antigua to San Juan La Laguna.
- **11:30–13:00** Check in, lunch.
- **13:00–17:30** 🚶 **Free: wander San Juan La Laguna and enjoy the lake.** Nothing is booked; it's a recovery afternoon after Acatenango. Ideas, all on foot and in no particular order:
  - Drop into a **weaving cooperative** to see backstrap weaving and natural dyes: [MayAchik'](https://livinginguatemala.com/activities/san-juan-la-laguna-textile-tour/) does a free 20-minute tour (paid workshops from Q65 (€7.7)); Artesanos de San Juan, Casa Flor Ixcaco and Mujeres en Colores are others.
  - Browse the **galleries of Tz'utujil painters**, whose bright, flat "naïve" style shows village life and the lake from above.
  - Walk the **colourful, mural-painted lanes** between the dock and the main square.
  - A **coffee tour or tasting** at the Cooperativa La Voz, or just a coffee by the water.
  - Climb to the **[Mirador Kaqasiiwaan](https://nomadsbeyond.com/san-juan-la-laguna-mirador/)** above the village (10–20 min up; Q20–Q30 (€2.4–€3.6), sources disagree) for the view to the volcanoes; it's a sunset spot, so time it for ~17:00.
  - 🏊 A **first swim** from your lodge's dock or a lakeside spot away from the village docks.

Collect the car from your hotel's parking and **drive about 3 hours from Antigua to Lake Atitlán**: via the CA-1 highway and Santa Clara La Laguna, down to
**[San Juan La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_La_Laguna)**. The final descent from Santa Clara to San Juan is very steep: use a low gear going down, and expect a small engine to labour on the way back up ([§1.3](#13-booking-checklist) explains the car choice). Park at your lodge; the car stays put until the 20th, because around the lake you get about on foot, by tuk-tuk and by boat.

**Linguistics:** San Juan and San Pedro speak Tz'utujil, a Maya language, but the lake's name *Atitlán* is Nahuatl, built on *atl* "water", the same root as in *axolotl*.

#### Fri 18 Dec — Indian Nose at sunrise, then swimming 🏊
**The day's plan**
- **04:00–08:00** 🌄 Indian Nose (Rostro Maya) sunrise hike, with a community guide or tour.
- **08:00–12:30** 🛌 Free: breakfast, a nap and a slow morning in San Juan.
- **12:30–13:00** ⛵ Lancha from the San Juan dock to San Marcos.
- **13:00–17:00** 🏊 Swim and jump at Cerro Tzankujil (open until 17:00).
- **~17:00** ⛵ Lancha back before the last boats (~17:00–18:00).
- **Evening:** dinner in San Juan.

- **[Indian Nose (Rostro Maya)](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Indian+Nose+Rostro+Maya+mirador+Santa+Clara+La+Laguna), 2,550 m.** Start around 04:00. It's 30–45 minutes up a short, steep path, then sunrise over the three volcanoes. The community fee is about Q25–Q50 (€3.0–€5.9); a local guide is optional at Q50–Q100 (€5.9–€12). Bring cash: there are **no ATMs in Santa Clara or San Juan**.
  - Pre-booked options (with transport and fees): **Turisteando**, Q222 (€26; listed as US$29), from San Juan ([book on WeTravel](https://turisteando.wetravel.com/trips/rostro-maya-indian-nose-hike-san-juan-la-laguna-turisteando-7327314571)). **Tours With Vick**, Q344 (€41; listed as US$45), rated 9.6/10 "Exceptional" on Travelocity ([book](https://www.travelocity.com/things-to-do/sunrise-hike-tour-on-indians-nose-mountain.a44904460.activity-details)).
  - 🇪🇸 **Spanish tip:** go to the trailhead yourselves and hire a community guide in Spanish for a fraction of the tour price.
- **Afternoon: [Cerro Tzankujil](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Reserva+Natural+Cerro+Tzankujil+San+Marcos+La+Laguna), San Marcos.** ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/cerro-tzankujil/1360016)) This sacred hill is now a nature reserve, 5 minutes' walk from the San Marcos dock. To get there, take a lancha (boat) **from the San Juan or San Pedro dock to San Marcos** (about Q20–Q25 (€2.4–€3.0)).

#### Sat 19 Dec — [Volcán San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcán_San_Pedro) (3,020 m) ⛰️
**The day's plan**
- **~06:00** Tuk-tuk to the park office in San Pedro La Laguna, register, meet the guide.
- **06:00–15:00** ⛰ Climb Volcán San Pedro and come back down (4–5 h up, 3–4 h down). *Or the swap:* 🚲 the lake-villages bike ride (box below).
- **15:30–17:00** 🏊 Cool-down swim in the lake.
- **Evening:** settle the lodge bill in cash, pack, check for blockades on the CA-1, early night. You leave at 07:00 tomorrow.

Start early. The climb gains about 1,200 m through coffee farms and cloud forest and takes **4–5 hours up and 3–4 hours down**, longer than I estimated before. You register at the [park office](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Parque+Ecol%C3%B3gico+Volc%C3%A1n+San+Pedro+La+Laguna) on the north-west edge of [San Pedro La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_La_Laguna). **Entry is Q100 (€12) and a guide is Q150–Q200 (€18–€24)** (the office assigns you one, and Spanish-speaking guides are the norm). In the afternoon, have a cool lake swim to finish.

> 🚲 **Swap option: cycle the lake villages instead of climbing San Pedro.** A gentle, mostly road ride with a swim stop, and a rest day for your legs between Acatenango and Tajumulco.
>
> - **Route:** about 5 km of dirt road to San Marcos, then paved road to San Pablo, with a stop at a lakeside beach to swim, then on to San Juan (weaving cooperatives, coffee) and San Pedro. You'd be riding through your own base.

Ask whether they'll pick you up in San Juan instead.
> - **On your own:** rent bikes in San Pedro or San Juan and ride San Juan → San Pablo → San Marcos → back. I'm only about 70 % sure rentals are easy to find; ask at your hostel. Ride by day only; this road links villages that you otherwise reach by boat ([§3.2](#32-what-to-avoid)).
> - **Not suitable:** the descent from Santa Clara down to San Juan (very steep, narrow), and the singletrack mountain-bike tours above the lake.

> 🏊 **Swimming in Atitlán:** the water is fresh and cool, probably around 20 °C (I'm about 70 % sure of that figure). Swim at designated spots like Tzankujil, away from village docks; the lake has had algae and pollution problems near towns.

### 2.3 Chichicastenango (20 Dec)

![Iglesia de Santo Tomás, Chichicastenango](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Iglesia_de_Santo_Tom%C3%A1s_2009.JPG?width=1280)
*Santo Tomás church. Its steps are still used for Maya ceremonies with copal incense.*

**What it is.** [Chichicastenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichicastenango) (Chichi) is a K'iche' Maya town with one of Central America's biggest markets every **Thursday and Sunday**. The white [church of Santo Tomás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_de_Santo_Tomás) was built in the 16th century on top of a pre-Hispanic temple platform. Its steps are still sacred: people burn copal incense and leave flower offerings on them.

> **👥 Crowds & 🚗 traffic here**
>
> - 👥👥 **This is the most crowded stop on the trip, on purpose.** Sunday market day plus festival week means packed alleys, tour buses from Antigua and Panajachel (they arrive around 10:00–14:00), firecrackers and pickpockets. I'd count it as one of your "special occasion" exceptions. If you'd rather skip it, drive straight from Atitlán to Xela (about 2.5 h) and have a slower morning by the lake.
> - 🚗 Parking is scarce on market days. Use a guarded *parqueo* on the edge of town and walk in.

#### Is the Santo Tomás festival worth it if the *palo volador* doesn't interest you?
**It's not worth an overnight stay, but you'll catch it anyway.** The festival runs from 13 to 21 December, and the *palo volador* (men spinning down from a 30 m pole) is its headline act on the 21st. The rest of it is processions of the saint's image with cofradías (religious brotherhoods), masked dances such as the *Baile de la Conquista*, and fireworks in the square at night. That is good to see, but it also means bigger crowds, higher room prices and firecrackers all day.

Your Sunday the 20th falls on a market day *and* inside the festival week, so a half-day visit gives you the market plus the festival atmosphere at no extra cost. Then you drive on.

#### Sun 20 Dec — Lake Atitlán → Chichicastenango market → Xela
**The day's plan**
- **07:00–09:30** 🚗 Drive San Juan → Chichicastenango (~2.5 h).
- **09:30–13:30** 🛍 Sunday market, the Santo Tomás church steps and the colourful cemetery; festival atmosphere.
- **13:30–15:30** 🚗 Drive Chichicastenango → Xela (~2 h).
- **15:30–18:30** 🚶 Free: check in, then wander Xela's centre around Parque Centro América. If you want, drop by your Tajumulco operator's office to confirm details.
- **Evening:** dinner in the centre; taxi back after ~21:00.

Leave San Juan by about 07:00 and **drive ~2.5 h from Lake Atitlán to Chichicastenango**. Spend 09:30–13:30 at the market, church steps and cemetery, then **drive ~2 h from Chichicastenango to Xela**, arriving well before dark.

![Chichicastenango market](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Chichicastenango_market_2009.JPG?width=1280)

> **Etiquette:** ask before photographing people, especially during ceremonies, and enter the church only by the main door, never the side doors.

>
> - 🚶 **Distance (estimate):** 3–6 km walking, mostly the market and the cemetery ([day table](#11-the-trip-at-a-glance)).
> - 💵 **Cash:** bring small notes for the market stalls and the guarded *parqueo* (car park). Bargain, but keep it friendly ([§3.1](#31-getting-by-in-guatemala)).
> - 📷 **Photos:** ask before photographing people, and **never photograph inside the Santo Tomás church** ([§3.1](#31-getting-by-in-guatemala)).

### 2.4 Xela & Tajumulco (20–24 Dec)

![Map of the highlands section]({{ "/maps/map-02-highlands.png" | relative_url }})

![Map of the area around Xela, with San Marcos Department hatched]({{ "/maps/map-05-xela.png" | relative_url }})

**What it is.** [Xela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetzaltenango) ("SHEH-la") is Guatemala's second city, at 2,330 m. It's cooler and much less touristy than Antigua, and full of Spanish schools and serious hiking operators. The nickname comes from its K'iche' name, *Xelajú*, usually glossed "under the ten (mountains)". Its official name, Quetzaltenango, is Nahuatl ([§1.2](#12-guatemala-in-brief)).

> **👥 Crowds & 🚗 traffic here**
>
> - 🚗 **Xela is a real city:** traffic, asphalt and exhaust in the centre and on the ring roads. The historic centre around Parque Centro América is pleasant on foot.
> - 🚗 **Cuatro Caminos** (the junction you pass on 20 and 24 Dec) is a busy truck-and-bus crossroads.
> - 👥 **Fuentes Georginas** gets local family crowds at weekends and holidays. Monday the 21st should be calmer; arrive after lunch, once the morning tours have gone.
> - 👥 **Tajumulco** is far less crowded than Acatenango: a few groups at camp, no huts.

#### Mon 21 Dec — Laguna Chicabal + Fuentes Georginas 🏊

![Laguna Chicabal crater lake](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Volcan_y_laguna_chicabal.jpg?width=1280)
*Laguna Chicabal, a crater lake sacred to the Mam Maya.*

**The day's plan**
- **~07:15** 🚗 Drive from Xela to the Chicabal entrance near San Martín Sacatepéquez (~45 min, my estimate).
- **08:00–12:00** ⛰ Laguna Chicabal: walk up, round the crater lake, back down (open 08:00–13:00).
- **12:00–13:30** 🚗 Drive towards Zunil, lunch on the way.
- **13:30–17:00** ♨ Fuentes Georginas hot springs, or a private steam sauna at Las Cumbres.
- **18:00–19:00** 📋 Tajumulco briefing in Xela (Quetzaltrekkers only).
- **Evening:** dinner, pack a small bag for Tajumulco, leave the big bags at the Xela hotel.

- **Morning: [Laguna Chicabal](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Laguna+Chicabal+San+Martin+Sacatepequez) (2,712 m).** ([volcano on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicabal)) This misty, emerald crater lake is **sacred to the Mam Maya; swimming is forbidden**, and you'll see altars along the shore. **It's open only 08:00–13:00**, so go first thing. Entry is Q20 (€2.4) for foreigners. The walk up from the entrance takes roughly 1–1.5 h each way.
- **Afternoon: [Fuentes Georginas](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Fuentes+Georginas+Zunil) hot springs.** These are steaming volcanic pools in cloud forest above Zunil. **Entry is Q60 (€7.1) per adult plus Q25 (€3.0) to park, cash only.** Bring a swimsuit, sandals and a towel.
  - **Private alternative, same village: [Las Cumbres](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Las+Cumbres+Eco-Hotel+Termalismo+y+Spa+Zunil)** (Eco-Hotel Termalismo y Spa, Zunil). Instead of public pools, it has **private saunas heated by natural volcanic steam**, each a closed room for up to 4 people with its own cold shower, so you can use it however you like, including without swimsuits. Overlanders reported Q75 (€8.9) per person for 1 hour (date of that price unknown; ask). Some of its rooms also have their own thermal-water tub. You could do both: the forest pools first, then a private hour here ([§3.8](#38-naturism)).

#### Tue 22 Dec — [Tajumulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcán_Tajumulco), day 1: hike up to camp ⛰️
**The day's plan**
- **05:00** Leave Xela with your operator (Quetzaltrekkers start at 05:00; others per booking).
- **~05:00–08:00** 🚌 Transport to the trailhead at Tuichán (~3,000 m).
- **~08:00–14:00** ⛰ Hike up to the camp near the summit, at about 4,000 m.
- **Afternoon and evening at camp:** rest, eat, stay warm. Nothing else is planned; it's cold up here. Early night for the pre-dawn summit.

If Tajumulco is off (see the boxes below), this day becomes a free day in Xela and the next morning is the Santa María sunrise hike instead.


![Volcán Tajumulco](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Tajumulco_Volcano.JPG?width=1280)
*Tajumulco, the highest point in Central America.*

The trek starts at about 3,000 m at Tuichán, 2–3 h from Xela. You camp near the summit and walk up for sunrise, often above a sea of cloud. After nine days at altitude you'll be well acclimatised. In Mam the name means "above the clouds".

> 🛑 **Read before booking Tajumulco: official travel advice.** Tajumulco lies in **San Marcos Department**. Since March 2026 the **US State Department has rated San Marcos "Level 4: Do Not Travel"** because of cartel and gang crime, and its staff may not go there at all. The **UK FCDO** advises against travel only within 5 km of the Mexican border, a strip Tajumulco is outside of. **Check your own government's advice and your travel insurance** (some policies don't cover areas under a do-not-travel warning) before choosing. **Plan B, Volcán Santa María (3,772 m), is in Quetzaltenango Department, right above Xela, and has no such warning.** It's a hard 1,500 m climb, best done as a pre-dawn hike for sunrise, looking down on the erupting Santiaguito dome. Quetzaltrekkers and Adrenalina both run it.

> ⚠️ **Access check.** Another Xela operator ([Monte Verde Tours](https://monteverdetours.rezdy.com/catalog/388612/all-hikes)) lists its Tajumulco trek as unavailable because of conflicts between neighbouring communities, and offers Santa María instead. Quetzaltrekkers says its Tajumulco treks are back and bookings are open. **Confirm with your operator a week ahead.** If it's off, the backup is **[Volcán Santa María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_María_(volcano)) (3,772 m)**: a one-day sunrise hike from Xela, with a view down onto the constantly erupting Santiaguito dome.

🇪🇸 **Spanish tip:** both operators run in Spanish. Adrenalina's [office](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Adrenalina+Tours+Quetzaltenango) is just off Parque Centro América, if you want to sort out details in person on the 20th.

#### Wed 23 Dec — Tajumulco, day 2: summit at sunrise, back to Xela ⛰️
**The day's plan**
- **~04:00–06:30** ⛰ Summit of Tajumulco (4,220 m) for sunrise, the highest point in Central America, often above a sea of cloud.
- **~06:30–11:00** ⛰ Back to camp, breakfast, descend to Tuichán.
- **~11:00–14:00** 🚌 Transport back to Xela.
- **14:00–18:00** 🛌 Free: hot shower, nap, laundry, a late lunch. Nothing planned.
- **Evening:** an easy dinner in the centre. Fill the tank and check for blockades on the CA-1, because tomorrow you leave at 06:00. Early night.

#### Thu 24 Dec — Xela → Cobán (~8 h)
**The day's plan**
- **06:00–14:00** 🚗 Drive Xela → Cobán (~8 h with stops), round Guatemala City.
- **14:00–18:00** 🚶 Free: check in, then wander Cobán. Climb the 130 steps to the **[Templo El Calvario](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/templo-el-calvario/482630)** for the view over town, and 💵 **draw cash** for Lanquín and Semuc.
- **Evening:** 🎄 **Nochebuena.** Tamales and *ponche* for dinner if your hotel or a restaurant serves them (many places close or are booked out with family dinners, so ask your hotel ahead), then the town-wide fireworks at midnight.

**Leave Xela at 06:00 for Cobán.** The route runs CA-1 → Guatemala City ring road → CA-9 → El Rancho → CA-14 north to Cobán, aiming to arrive by about 14:00. Christmas Eve traffic around Guatemala City can be heavy, so don't dawdle. At night it's **Nochebuena**, the main Christmas celebration: families eat tamales and drink *ponche* (hot fruit punch), and at midnight the whole town sets off fireworks at once.

### 2.5 Cobán, Lanquín & Semuc Champey (24–27 Dec)

![Map of the north and east section]({{ "/maps/map-03-north.png" | relative_url }})

**What it is.** Alta Verapaz is green, rainy and mountainous. [Cobán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobán) is the hub;
**[Lanquín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanquín)** is a small Q'eqchi' town in a river valley, and the base for
**[Semuc Champey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semuc_Champey)**. Semuc is a 300 m natural limestone bridge over the Río Cahabón: the river vanishes underneath it, and on top sits a staircase of turquoise pools you can swim in.

> **👥 Crowds & 🚗 traffic here**
>
> - 👥 **Semuc Champey** is very busy from about 10:30 to 15:00, when hostel tours arrive, and Christmas week brings domestic tourists too. Be at the gate at opening and swim before the crowds, or come back for the last two hours.
> - 👥 **K'anba cave** tours leave every half hour in groups. Take the first one of the day.
> - 🚗 **Cobán** is a busy town with narrow, congested streets. The **Cobán–Lanquín road** is winding with lots of pickups, but traffic is light.

![Semuc Champey pools](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Semuc_Champey%2C_Guatemala.jpg?width=1280)
*The pools at Semuc Champey.*

**Linguistics:** *Semuc Champey* is Q'eqchi', usually glossed "where the river hides under the stones/earth", which is exactly what you see.

#### Fri 25 Dec — Cobán → Lanquín (~2 h) 🏊
**The day's plan**
- **Early morning:** a slow Christmas breakfast in Cobán (the town will be quiet after the night's fireworks); top up cash if you didn't yesterday.
- **09:00–11:00** 🚗 Drive Cobán → Lanquín (~2 h, winding).
- **11:00–13:00** Check in at the lodge, lunch.
- **13:00–16:00** 🏊 River tubing on the Río Lanquín (arranged by your lodge, if it runs on Christmas Day).
- **17:00–18:30** 🦇 Grutas de Lanquín at dusk, to watch the bats stream out.
- **Evening:** dinner at the lodge. Don't drive after dark.

The last stretch **from Cobán to Lanquín** is a winding mountain road. In the afternoon, go **tubing on the river** (~Q65–Q100 (€7.7–€12) each, arranged by your hostel). At dusk, visit the
**[Grutas de Lanquín](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Grutas+de+Lanquin)** (Q30 (€3.6) for foreigners), where thousands of bats stream out of the cave mouth.
> 🎄 It's Christmas Day, so some operators may not run. Message your hostel a few days ahead.

#### Sat 26 Dec — Semuc Champey 🏊
**The day's plan**
- **~07:30** 🚙 Lodge 4×4 pickup from Lanquín to Semuc Champey; the car stays in Lanquín.
- **08:00–16:00** 🏊 At Semuc: K'anba cave first, then El Mirador, then the pools (early or late, to avoid the 10:30–15:00 crowds).
- **~16:00** 🚙 Pickup back to Lanquín.
- **Evening:** 🛌 free, at the lodge. Pack; you leave at 06:30 tomorrow.

- **Park entry:** sources disagree. Q50 (€5.9) for foreigners is the older figure, and one current tour listing says Q110 (€13). **Bring Q110 (€13) each in cash.**
- **Leave the car in Lanquín.** The last ~10 km **from Lanquín to the Semuc Champey entrance** are very rough, so take the hostels' 4×4 pickups from Lanquín.
- **Suggested day:** start with the **K'anba cave** (candlelit, partly swimming, with a rope swing and waterfall). Then climb to **El Mirador** (30–40 min, steep) for the classic view from above. Finish by swimming in the pools, before the afternoon crowds or after them.

**The day's plan**

### 2.6 Tikal & Yaxhá (27–29 Dec)

![Temple I at Tikal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Tikal_Temple_I.jpg?width=1280)
*Temple I ("Temple of the Great Jaguar"), the tomb of king Jasaw Chan K'awiil, who died in 734.*

**What it is.** [Tikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikal) was one of the greatest Classic Maya cities. Its steep temples rise above the rainforest canopy, and the jungle around them is alive with spider and howler monkeys, toucans and coatis.
**[Yaxhá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaxha)** is smaller and much quieter, between two lagoons, and its Temple 216 gives one of the best sunsets in Guatemala.

> **👥 Crowds & 🚗 traffic here**
>
> - 👥 **Tikal's Gran Plaza and Temple IV** fill up with tour groups from about 09:30 to 14:00, more so in the Christmas–New Year week. Arriving at 06:00 gives you 2–3 calm hours. The outer groups (Mundo Perdido, Temple VI) stay quiet all day.
> - 👥 **Yaxhá** is quiet except at sunset on Temple 216, where 30–80 people gather. It's still nothing like Tikal.
> - 🚗 **Flores / Santa Elena:** tuk-tuks, traffic and a busy causeway. Your base, El Remate, is a quiet village.

![Map of Tikal, Yaxhá and Lake Petén Itzá]({{ "/maps/map-06-peten.png" | relative_url }})

**Linguistics:** "Tikal" is a modern name, often glossed as Yucatec Maya for "at the water hole". The city's own name, read from its hieroglyphs, was *Yax Mutal*.

#### Mon 28 Dec — Tikal at opening → Yaxhá at sunset
**The day's plan**
- **~05:20** 🚗 Leave El Remate for Tikal (~35 min).
- **06:00–12:30** 🏛 Tikal: Temple IV, Gran Plaza, Mundo Perdido, Central Acropolis.
- **12:30–14:30** 🚗 Lunch, then drive Tikal → Yaxhá (~1.5 h, last 11 km dirt). Check in at Ecolodge El Sombrero.

## Part 3 · Practical

### 3.1 Getting by in Guatemala

#### Money
- **The currency is the quetzal (Q).** At the time of writing, €1 ≈ Q8.43. Carry **small notes** (Q5–Q50). Tuk-tuks, boats, market stalls and park gates often can't change a Q200 note.
- **Cash is essential:** San Juan and Santa Clara have no ATMs ([§2.2](#22-lake-atitlán-1720-dec)), Tikal has none ([§2.6](#26-tikal--yaxhá-2729-dec)), Lanquín may not have a reliable one ([§2.5](#25-cobán-lanquín--semuc-champey-2427-dec)), and many park gates and lanchas take cash only. Draw money in Antigua ([§2.1](#21-antigua--acatenango-1317-dec)), Xela ([§2.4](#24-xela--tajumulco-2024-dec)), Cobán ([§2.5](#25-cobán-lanquín--semuc-champey-2427-dec)) and Flores ([§2.6](#26-tikal--yaxhá-2729-dec)). Panajachel has ATMs too, but your route only passes near it. I'm about 80 % sure Río Dulce town has bank ATMs ([§2.7](#27-río-dulce--the-drive-home-2931-dec)).
- **Use ATMs inside banks or shopping centres** during opening hours, not stand-alone street machines, where card skimming is more common.
- **Tipping:** about 10 % in sit-down restaurants (check whether *propina* is already on the bill). For hiking guides (Acatenango [§2.1](#21-antigua--acatenango-1317-dec), Tajumulco [§2.4](#24-xela--tajumulco-2024-dec)), Q50–Q100 (€5.9–€12) per person per day is usual. Round up for drivers.

#### Language & people
- Your Spanish will make a huge difference. Use *usted* with older people and officials. In the highlands, many people speak Spanish as a second language after a Mayan language, so speak clearly and simply.
- Greet people when you walk into a shop or a room (*buenos días* / *buenas tardes*). It matters here.
- **Ask before photographing people**, especially women, children and ceremonies, and never photograph inside the Santo Tomás church in Chichicastenango ([§2.3](#23-chichicastenango-20-dec)). Some people will ask for a small payment for a photo, which is fair.
- **Bargaining:** it's normal in markets (Chichi [§2.3](#23-chichicastenango-20-dec), Antigua's handicraft market [§2.1](#21-antigua--acatenango-1317-dec)), but keep it friendly. Don't haggle in shops with fixed prices, or over a few quetzales with a weaver.

#### Food & water
- **Don't drink tap water**, and don't use it to brush your teeth. Most hotels and hostels have big refill jugs, so bring a refillable bottle.
- Street food is fine if it's **hot, cooked in front of you and busy**. Be careful with raw salads and unpeeled fruit, and with ice outside tourist restaurants.
- Try *pepián* (a spiced meat stew), *kak'ik* (Q'eqchi' turkey soup, a Cobán speciality), *tamales* (especially on Christmas Eve), *chuchitos*, *rellenitos* (sweet plantain), and *atol* (a hot corn drink).

#### Everyday practicalities
- **Toilet paper goes in the bin, not the toilet**, almost everywhere. Carry your own paper and hand gel; public toilets often charge Q1–Q3 (€0.1–€0.4).
- Get a local **SIM or eSIM** (Tigo and Claro are the two networks) and use **WhatsApp**, which is how hotels, guides and tour operators communicate.
- Download **offline maps** (Google Maps or Maps.me) and **Waze**. Mobile signal disappears on parts of the Lanquín and Sayaxché roads.
- Electricity is 120 V with US-style plugs (types A/B), so European travellers need adapters.

### 3.2 What to avoid
- **Driving at night:** see [§3.5](#35-driving).
- **Zone 18 of Guatemala City, the city of Villa Nueva and Zone 3** are high-crime areas. Your route never goes there; you're in the city only on the evening of 30 Dec ([§2.7](#27-río-dulce--the-drive-home-2931-dec)).
- **Chicken buses and street-hailed white taxis.** They're colourful, but US government staff are banned from using both because of crime and accidents. Use Uber in Guatemala City and Antigua, taxis your hotel calls for you, and tuk-tuks in towns.
- **Walking between Lake Atitlán villages on lakeside trails** without a guide: these isolated paths have a history of robberies. Take the boats ([§2.2](#22-lake-atitlán-1720-dec)).
- **Showing off valuables:** keep your phone away on busy streets, leave jewellery at home, and carry a day's cash in a front pocket.
- **Illegal drugs:** the penalties are harsh, and the trade behind them is violent.
- **Hikes on Volcán de Agua or Pacaya without a reputable guide:** armed robberies of hikers have been reported there.
- **Getting into political protests or road blockades** (*bloqueos*). If you meet one, turn round and wait it out.
- **Feeding or touching dogs:** rabies is present, and post-exposure vaccine is only available in larger city hospitals.

### 3.3 Health

> I'm not a doctor. **Book a travel-clinic appointment now.** Your trip is 3 weeks away, and some vaccines need time to take effect. The clinic can also prescribe the antibiotic marked ℞ below (℞ = needs a prescription).

#### Vaccines
- **Recommended for most travellers (CDC):** hepatitis A and typhoid, plus routine vaccines (MMR, tetanus–diphtheria–pertussis, polio). Travel clinics also often suggest hepatitis B.
- **Rabies:** consider it, since you'll be in rural areas and around dogs. In Guatemala, treatment after a bite is only available in larger city hospitals.
- **No vaccine is required for entry** if you're coming directly from Europe or the US.

#### Mosquitoes: dengue, malaria
- **Dengue:** Guatemala had record dengue numbers in 2024. **December is past the peak but not risk-free.** Cases peak in July–October with the rains, and December is the tail end of the higher-risk period (roughly May–December), with the dry season bringing fewer breeding sites. Dengue circulates all year in warm, humid lowlands, and you're there from 25 to 30 Dec. The highlands (Antigua, Atitlán, Xela) are too cool for much transmission. So: repellent by day in the lowlands, and no reason to change the plan. The mosquitoes bite **by day**, mostly in the lowlands and towns: Lanquín and Semuc ([§2.5](#25-cobán-lanquín--semuc-champey-2427-dec)), Petén ([§2.6](#26-tikal--yaxhá-2729-dec)), Río Dulce ([§2.7](#27-río-dulce--the-drive-home-2931-dec)). Use DEET or picaridin repellent and wear long sleeves at dusk in the lowlands.
- **Malaria:** CDC says there's no risk in Antigua, Guatemala City or around Lake Atitlán, and only low risk in rural lowlands such as Petén. For your route, CDC advises **bite prevention rather than pills**. Confirm this with your clinic.

#### Altitude sickness: the real risk on this trip
Altitude sickness (doctors call the common form *acute mountain sickness*, or AMS) is **the most likely health problem on your itinerary**, because you sleep at ~3,600 m on Acatenango ([§2.1](#21-antigua--acatenango-1317-dec)) and ~4,000 m on Tajumulco or Santa María ([§2.4](#24-xela--tajumulco-2024-dec)). Above about 2,500 m, symptoms are common.

**How much should you worry?** For fit people in their 20s and 30s, expect it to be **a nuisance rather than a danger**: a headache, poor sleep and low appetite at camp, gone after you descend the next morning. Two caveats:
- **Fitness doesn't protect you.** It makes the climb easier, but who gets AMS depends mostly on **how fast you gain height** and on personal susceptibility. Fit people who push hard on the way up get it as often as anyone, sometimes more.
- **Acatenango is the bigger jump.** On day 3 you go from sleeping at 1,530 m to sleeping at ~3,600 m. Mild symptoms there are very common. Tajumulco comes after 3 nights at 2,330 m in Xela, so you'll be better adapted.

**What to do:**
- Go **slowly on the way up** (the guides set the pace), drink 3–4 L of water, and skip alcohol the night before and at camp.
- **Use your altitude medication as your doctor prescribed**: either preventively from the day before the climb, or at the first symptoms, depending on what they told you. Bring painkillers for headaches too.
- 🚩 **Descend straight away if you get:** a headache that painkillers don't fix, vomiting, unusual clumsiness or confusion (walking "drunk"), or breathlessness at rest or a wet cough. These are signs of the rare but serious forms: HACE (high-altitude cerebral oedema, fluid on the brain) and HAPE (high-altitude pulmonary oedema, fluid in the lungs). **Going down is the treatment.** On both volcanoes you can be 1,000 m lower within 2–3 hours, and guides are trained for this.
- Climbing to the summit is optional. If one of you feels bad at camp, staying put or descending early is a perfectly good outcome.

#### Suggested medicine kit (for two people)
Brand names vary by country; the generic (active-ingredient) name is the one to ask for at a pharmacy.

| Item | What it is | When to use it on this trip |
|---|---|---|
| **Your regular prescriptions**, plus copies of the prescriptions | Whatever you normally take | Pharmacies may not carry the same brands, and a copy helps at customs |
| **Paracetamol** (called *acetaminophen* in the US; brands Panadol, Tylenol) | A painkiller and fever reducer | Altitude headaches, aches, fever. Gentle on the stomach. |

**Hospitals:** the best private hospitals are in Guatemala City (e.g. Hospital Herrera Llerandi, Centro Médico) and Antigua (Hospital Privado Hermano Pedro). **Get travel insurance that covers medical evacuation and volcano hiking.**

### 3.5 Driving

- **Check for road blockades** (*bloqueos*) the evening before each long drive, by asking your hotel or searching "bloqueos" with the route name. The UK FCDO has noted a state of emergency in **Nahualá and Santa Catarina Ixtahuacán (Sololá)**, a long-running land dispute next to the **CA-1 highway you use on 17 ([§2.1](#21-antigua--acatenango-1317-dec)), 20 ([§2.3](#23-chichicastenango-20-dec)) and 24 Dec ([§2.4](#24-xela--tajumulco-2024-dec))**. Ask locally whether that stretch is calm.
- **Night driving:** unlit roads, unmarked speed bumps, unlit vehicles and the risk of crime are why every drive in this guide ends by about 17:00, except the 1 h arrival drive on 13 Dec on main roads ([§2.1](#21-antigua--acatenango-1317-dec)). Sunset is around 17:30.
- **Leave nothing visible in a parked car.** Break-ins are the most common problem for drivers. Use guarded parking (*parqueo*) or your hotel's own car park; for Antigua's costs and street-parking rules, see [Parking in Antigua](#parking-in-antigua-1317-dec).
- **Watch for unmarked speed bumps** (*túmulos*) and aggressive overtaking.
- **Use Waze as well as Google Maps**, because Google sometimes routes you onto dirt roads.
- **Carry your passport, driving licence, rental papers and an International Driving Permit.** Police checkpoints are common.
- **Bring plenty of cash in quetzales.** San Juan, Santa Clara, Tikal and many park gates have no ATM or don't take cards; see [§3.1](#31-getting-by-in-guatemala) for where to draw money.
- **Altitude:** see [§3.3](#33-health).
- **Holidays:** expect closures and busy roads on 24–25 Dec and 31 Dec.

### 3.8 Naturism

**Short answer: there are no nudist or clothing-optional beaches in Guatemala, and nude bathing in public places isn't tolerated.** It's a conservative, largely religious country, and the Maya communities around your route are more conservative still. Sandee's overview of Guatemala's nudism rules finds no officially recognised nudist beaches and notes that Lake Atitlán, in particular, doesn't permit nude bathing, with local villages posting signs asking visitors not to walk around undressed. I couldn't confirm which exact legal provision applies, but the penal code has offences against public decency, so treat public nudity as a real risk of a fine or detention, not just disapproval.

**What does work: private spaces.**

| Where | Date | What | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Las Cumbres](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Las+Cumbres+Eco-Hotel+Termalismo+y+Spa+Zunil), Zunil** ([§2.4](#24-xela--tajumulco-2024-dec)) | 21 Dec, after or instead of Fuentes Georginas | Private rooms with saunas heated by natural volcanic steam, each for up to 4 people, with a cold shower | about Q75 (€8.9) per person per hour (older report; ask) |
| **Your own lodging** | any night | Book a place with a **private** pool, hot tub or walled terrace (filter Booking.com or Airbnb for "private pool" / "hot tub"), and check that it isn't overlooked | varies |
| **Temazcal** (Maya sweat lodge) | Atitlán, 17–19 Dec | Traditional sweat bath, often offered as a private session around San Marcos La Laguna; ask whether a private booking is possible. I'm about 60 % sure of how easy this is to find in December | ask locally |

Naturist-specific sites such as [Naturist BnB](https://naturistbnb.com) list few or no places in Guatemala; check before relying on them. The nearest legal nude beach is **Zipolite** in Oaxaca, Mexico, far off your route.

**Remote river or waterfall spots:** even where nobody seems to be around, a farmer, fisher or family can appear at any time, and many water spots are sacred or community-owned. I'd not risk it.
