# Guatemala itinerary · 13–31 December 2026

> **Version 1, written 22 November 2026.** This is the first draft from the
> chat: a driving loop through the highlands, Semuc Champey and Tikal. It was
> written before the arrival and departure times were known.

Assumptions: you fly in and out of Guatemala City (GUA), you're both fit enough for overnight hikes at high altitude, and you rent a car at the airport. The route goes from lower to higher ground so your bodies adjust before the hardest climb. It also puts you in Chichicastenango on Dec 20–21 for its biggest festival of the year, then finishes in the jungle up north.

## Day by day

**Dec 13 (Sun) – Arrive, drive to Antigua (about 1–1.5 h).** Pick up the car at the airport. If you land after about 16:00, sleep near the airport and drive in the morning.

**Dec 14 – Antigua (1,530 m).** Walk the town and climb Cerro de la Cruz. Book anything you haven't booked yet. This is an easy day to start adjusting to the altitude.

**Dec 15–16 – Acatenango overnight.** You camp facing Fuego and watch it erupt at night. The climb is demanding: 5–7 hours of continuous ascent gaining 1,500 vertical metres to camp, with the 3,976 m summit at dawn. Tours pick you up in Antigua, so the car stays parked. Book now: advance booking is essential in peak season (December–April). **Check the status before you go.** The trail was suspended in early August 2026 after a strong Fuego eruption, and it reopened to tourists on 7 August 2026. Official alerts: https://insivumeh.gob.gt

**Dec 17 – Rest morning, drive to Lake Atitlán (about 2.5–3 h).** I'd stay in San Juan La Laguna or San Pedro, which you can reach by road.

**Dec 18 – Indian Nose (Rostro Maya) at sunrise.** It's a short, steep climb from San Juan with the best view of the lake. In the afternoon, kayak or visit the weaving cooperatives.

**Dec 19 – San Pedro volcano (about 3,020 m).** Plan 4–6 hours round trip with a local guide.

**Dec 20 (Sun) – Drive to Chichicastenango (about 2–2.5 h).** You'll catch the Sunday market and the eve of the festival. Book a room in Chichi now.

**Dec 21 (Mon) – The Santo Tomás festival's main day.** The saint's day is 21 December; the full party starts two days earlier, with processions, music, dancing and a fair. The highlight is the *palo volador*: 30 m wooden poles are set up in the square near the Church of Santo Tomás, and dancers spin down on ropes from the top. If you enter the church, don't use the side doors. Drive to Xela (about 2 h) after lunch so you arrive before dark.

**Dec 22–23 – Tajumulco overnight, Central America's highest point (4,220 m).** By now you'll have spent over a week at altitude. Guides advise spending at least 2–3 days above 2,000 m before attempting Tajumulco. The hike climbs from 3,000 m at the trailhead to the summit at 4,220 m. Quetzaltrekkers in Xela is a good operator; it's run by volunteers and its profits go to a children's charity. One trip costs GTQ 800, rising to GTQ 850 on 1 January 2027. https://www.quetzaltrekkers.com/xela/tajumulco/

**Dec 24 – Laguna Chicabal and Fuentes Georginas.** In the morning, take an easy hike to the sacred crater lake. In the afternoon, soak your legs in the hot springs. That night is Nochebuena: tamales at home and a citywide burst of fireworks at midnight.

**Dec 25 – Long drive to Cobán (about 7 h via Guatemala City).** Christmas Day usually has light traffic.

**Dec 26 – Semuc Champey.** A chain of turquoise limestone pools, with a steep 30–40 minute climb to the El Mirador viewpoint. The last stretch past Lanquín is very rough, so park in Lanquín and take a local pickup truck.

**Dec 27 – Drive Lanquín → Flores via Sayaxché (about 6 h, including a short car ferry).**

**Dec 28 – Tikal.** Spend the whole day there, and go at sunrise if you can.

**Dec 29 – Yaxhá.** Quieter than Tikal; the sunset from Temple 216 over the lagoons is excellent.

**Dec 30 – Get back to Guatemala City.** Drive (about 8–9 h), or drop the car in Flores and take the 1-hour flight. I'm only about 60% sure that one-way drop-off at Flores is offered everywhere, so ask your rental company before booking.

**Dec 31 – Fly home.**

## Driving notes

- **Rent a high-clearance SUV, ideally 4×4.** Many roads to trailheads and natural sites are unpaved, steep, rocky or rutted, especially the Lanquín and Sayaxché stretches.
- **Don't drive after dark.** Rural highways have no lighting.
- **Leave nothing visible in the parked car.** Break-ins are the single most common problem for travellers who drive. Pay for guarded parking (*parqueo*).
- **Watch for unmarked speed bumps** (*túmulos*) and aggressive overtaking.
- **Use Waze as well as Google Maps.** Google Maps sometimes routes you onto dirt roads.
- **Get the rental company's full insurance**, and check whether your credit card's cover applies in Guatemala.
- **Carry your documents:** passport, licence, rental papers and an International Driving Permit.

## Too much driving?

For a trip built more around hiking, you could cut Dec 25–30 (Cobán, Semuc and Petén). Instead, drive 2–3 hours north of Xela to Todos Santos Cuchumatán and spend 3–4 days walking the Cuchumatanes. Then return to Antigua and climb Pacaya or Agua on the 30th. That version roughly halves the driving.

**A bit of linguistics:** "Xela" is short for K'iche' *Xelajú*, usually glossed "under the ten (mountains)". Chichicastenango comes from Nahuatl and roughly means "place of the nettles". Tajumulco is said to mean "above the clouds" in Mam. Even *quetzal*, the national bird and the currency, comes from Nahuatl *quetzalli*, "long tail feather".

Book soon, since it's peak season: the Acatenango tour, your Chichi room for Dec 20, and Tikal-area lodging over Christmas.
